Who needs a lumbar pillow

Your lower spine has a natural inward curve. Many dining chairs, budget office chairs, and even some gaming chairs leave a gap behind that curve, so after a while you slump forward to fill it. A lumbar support pillow fills the gap and gives your lower back something to rest against. It costs a small fraction of a new ergonomic chair, which makes it a sensible first step if your current chair is otherwise fine.

If your chair already has adjustable lumbar support, try adjusting that first. A pillow on top of good built-in support can push you too far forward on the seat.

What to look for

  • Shape — most pillows are a curved half-moon or a contoured rectangle. Contoured shapes follow the curve of your lower back; flat cushions mainly add padding.
  • Firmness — memory foam is the most common material. Softer foam feels comfortable at first but can flatten over a long day; firmer, denser foam holds its shape longer. Some pillows let you adjust firmness with an air bladder.
  • Size compared with your chair — measure the width and depth of your backrest. A thick pillow on a shallow seat leaves you perched on the front edge, and a wide pillow can overhang a narrow backrest.
  • Straps that stay put — slipping down the chair is the most common complaint about lumbar pillows. Look for an adjustable strap, or two, that wraps around the backrest, plus a grippy back panel.
  • Breathable, washable cover — a mesh cover helps if you run warm, and a removable, machine-washable cover keeps it clean.

Popular starting points

Memory-foam pillows from brands such as Everlasting Comfort and Qutool turn up again and again in buying guides at the budget end. Premium foam options, including Tempur-Pedic's lumbar cushion, cost more and tend to be firmer. Read recent reviews, since cover materials and strap designs change between versions.

Getting the position right

Sit all the way back in your chair, then place the thickest part of the pillow at the small of your back, around belt height. You should feel supported, not pushed forward. Pair it with a monitor at eye level and feet flat on the floor or on a footrest; a pillow can't make up for a screen that keeps pulling you forward.

Bottom line

For most people, a contoured, medium-firm memory-foam pillow with a secure strap is all you need. A pillow is a comfort aid, not a treatment. If you have ongoing back pain, see a doctor or physical therapist.
