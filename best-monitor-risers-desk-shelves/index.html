Riser or monitor arm

A monitor riser is a small platform or shelf that sits on your desk and lifts your screen by a few inches. A monitor arm clamps to the desk edge and lets you move the screen freely (see our monitor arm guide). Arms adjust more, but they need a desk edge they can clamp to and a monitor with VESA mounting holes. A riser needs neither. It works with any screen and any desk and installs in a minute, which is why it suits renters, shared desks, and anyone who just wants a quick fix for a screen that sits too low.

What to look for

  • Lift height — most risers raise a screen by roughly 3 to 6 inches. Ideally the top of your screen sits at or slightly below eye level. Sit normally, measure how far your current screen is from that height, and buy accordingly. Some risers offer adjustable leg heights.
  • Width and depth — measure your monitor's base and your desk. Single risers fit one screen; long shelves, often 35 inches or more, can hold two monitors or a monitor and a laptop. Make sure the shelf leaves enough desk depth in front for your keyboard.
  • Weight rating — check the stated capacity against your monitor's weight, especially for larger screens or if you plan to stack speakers or a laptop on it as well.
  • Material — steel or metal-mesh risers are slim and sturdy, and the vents let the shelf breathe. Wood or bamboo shelves look warmer on a home desk. Check that the legs are rigid, since a wobbly riser makes the screen shake as you type.
  • Storage — two-tier designs, drawers, and pen trays turn the riser into desk organization as well. Space underneath often fits a closed laptop or a keyboard you push out of the way at the end of the day.
  • Feet — non-slip pads stop the riser sliding and protect your desk surface.

Popular starting points

Steel risers from brands such as HUANUO and WALI turn up again and again in budget buying guides. Wide wood-and-steel shelves fit dual-monitor setups. Compare current listings for exact dimensions and weight ratings rather than relying on a brand name, since sizes vary a lot between models.

Pair it with the rest of your setup

A riser works best alongside a laptop stand if you use a laptop as a second screen, and a cable tray or clips to tidy the wires that now run down from a higher screen. A desk mat in front of the riser helps the area look like one finished setup.

Bottom line

If your screen sits too low and you don't want to drill or clamp anything, a sturdy riser of the right height and width is one of the cheapest ergonomic upgrades available. Measure first and buy for the lift and footprint you need.
