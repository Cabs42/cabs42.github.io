Who actually needs a footrest

Most desks are built at a fixed height of around 28 to 30 inches, and most office chairs are adjusted so your arms land at the right height for the keyboard. For shorter people, that often leaves the feet dangling or resting only on the toes. Without support, the front edge of the seat presses into the back of the thighs and people tend to slump forward. A footrest raises the "floor" to meet your feet so your knees can sit at roughly a 90-degree angle with your thighs level. If your feet already rest flat with the chair at the right height, you probably don't need one.

Types of footrest

  • Foam cushion footrests — high-density or memory foam with a removable, washable cover. Soft, quiet, and easy to slide around. Many have a two-part design that lets you change the height by adding or removing a layer, and some can flip over into a gentle rocking shape.
  • Rocking footrests — usually wood or plastic with a curved base. The rocking motion keeps your legs moving a little, which many people find helps with restlessness during long sitting stretches. Some double as a balance board when using a standing desk.
  • Adjustable-angle platforms — rigid plastic or metal platforms with a tilt that locks in place. Firmer than foam and the most precise to set up.

What to check before buying

  • Height range — measure the gap between the floor and the bottom of your feet when you're sitting correctly. Pick a footrest whose height (or adjustment range) covers that gap.
  • Surface size — wide enough to shift your feet around without sliding off the edge.
  • Non-slip base — especially on hard floors.
  • Cover — removable and washable covers last much longer on foam models.
  • Weight capacity — matters most for rocking and balance-board styles.

Practical setup tips

Set your chair height for your arms and keyboard first, then add the footrest to fill the gap underneath. Don't raise the footrest so high that your knees end up above your hips. Change your foot position through the day rather than locking into one posture.

Bottom line

A footrest is one of the cheapest ergonomic fixes there is. For most people a height-adjustable foam footrest with a washable cover is the easiest choice; pick a rocking model if you fidget or switch between sitting and standing.
