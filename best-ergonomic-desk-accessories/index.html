Posture is a setup problem, not a willpower problem

If your back or neck aches by mid-afternoon, the fix is rarely "sit up straighter." A setup that forces you to hunch will win every time. The better approach is to change the equipment so good posture becomes the natural, effortless position. A few targeted accessories do most of the work, and none of this is medical advice; it is about arranging your desk so your body is not fighting it.

Raise the screen to your eyes

The most common cause of neck strain is a monitor or laptop that sits too low, pulling your head and chin downward all day.

  • Monitor arm — a clamp-mounted arm lets you float the screen at the correct height and distance and frees up desk space underneath. A monitor arm is the standard recommendation here because it adjusts in every direction; models that handle larger and heavier panels exist for big displays.
  • Laptop stand — if you work on a laptop, raising the screen to eye level is the single biggest ergonomic win. Pair it with an external keyboard and mouse, because once the screen is up, you no longer want to reach down to the laptop's own keyboard.

Support the hands and wrists

  • External keyboard and mouse — these let you keep the screen high while your hands rest at a neutral, relaxed height.
  • Wrist rests — a padded rest for the keyboard and mouse keeps your wrists straight rather than bent upward, which is where a lot of strain hides.

Support the body

  • Footrest — if your feet do not sit flat on the floor when your chair is at the right height, a footrest restores a stable, supported base and takes pressure off your thighs and lower back.
  • Lumbar cushion — an inexpensive lumbar pillow fills the gap between your lower back and a chair that does not support it, helping you keep a neutral spine.

Put it together

Work top to bottom: get the screen to eye level first, then bring the keyboard and mouse to a neutral hand height, then support your feet and lower back. Each change is small, but together they remove the reasons you slump in the first place. If pain persists despite a well-arranged setup, it is worth talking to a professional.

Bottom line

You do not need an expensive chair to feel better at your desk. A monitor arm or laptop stand, an external keyboard and mouse, and simple back and foot support fix posture at the source for a modest total cost.
