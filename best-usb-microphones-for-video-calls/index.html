Why a separate microphone helps

On most work calls, people forgive a grainy picture long before they forgive bad audio. Laptop microphones sit far from your mouth, pick up keyboard clatter and fan noise, and make you sound like you are in a large empty room. A USB microphone placed close to you fixes most of that, and because it plugs straight into your computer there is no audio interface or extra software to deal with.

Dynamic or condenser

This is the most important choice, and it depends on your room.

  • Condenser microphones are sensitive and capture a lot of detail. In a quiet, furnished room they sound full and clear. In a room with a loud fan, a barking dog, or hard bare walls, they also capture all of that.
  • Dynamic microphones are less sensitive and mainly pick up what is directly in front of them. They need to sit closer to your mouth, but they reject far more background noise. For shared homes, open-plan spaces, or echoey rooms, a dynamic mic is usually the safer choice.

What else to look for

  • Pickup pattern — a cardioid pattern records what is in front of the mic and rejects sound from behind it, which is what you want for a solo caller. Some mics offer several patterns for interviews or two people at one desk, but most home office workers only ever use cardioid.
  • Mute button and gain control — a physical mute you can tap mid-call and a gain knob to set your level are both worth having.
  • Headphone jack — lets you hear yourself with no delay, which helps you set your level and spot noise problems.
  • USB and XLR outputs — some mics offer both. That matters only if you might later move into podcasting with an audio interface.
  • Mounting — a desk stand is fine to start with, but a boom arm lets you bring the mic close to your mouth while keeping it out of your camera's view.

Popular starting points

The Blue Yeti, now sold under Logitech's brand, is a long-running condenser choice with several selectable pickup patterns. For noisier rooms, the Samson Q2U is a widely recommended budget dynamic mic with both USB and XLR outputs, and the Shure MV7 line covers the premium end of USB dynamic mics. Compare current versions and reviews before buying, since models and prices change often.

Setup tips

Keep the mic about a hand's width or two from your mouth, slightly off to the side so you are not breathing straight into it. Select it as the input in your call app rather than relying on the system default, and soften a bare room with a rug or curtains if you sound echoey.

Bottom line

Pick a dynamic USB mic if your room is noisy, and a condenser if your room is quiet and you want the fullest sound. Either one, placed close to you, will sound far better than your laptop's built-in microphone.
