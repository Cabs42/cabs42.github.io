Why a laptop stand matters more than it looks

A laptop sitting flat on a desk puts the screen well below eye level, which means hours of looking down and a slowly aching neck. A stand fixes this by raising the screen to a natural viewing height. The catch: once the laptop is raised, its built-in keyboard and trackpad are too high and too far away to use comfortably, so a stand only delivers its ergonomic benefit when it is paired with a separate external keyboard and mouse.

What to look for

  • Height adjustability. A fixed-height riser is cheaper, but an adjustable stand lets you match the screen to your actual eye level, which varies by chair, desk, and how tall you are.
  • Stability. A wider, heavier base resists tipping when you type on an external keyboard placed in front of it. Read reviews for wobble complaints before buying, especially with larger laptops.
  • Ventilation. Look for an open frame or mesh design that lets air flow under the laptop rather than trapping heat against a solid surface.
  • Material. Aluminum stands are the sturdiest and best at dissipating heat. Folding stands in lighter materials trade some stability for portability if you also want to bring it to a coffee shop or the office.

Fixed riser vs. adjustable arm

A simple angled riser is the right call for most home desks: it is cheap, stable, and does the one job of lifting the screen. A fully articulating arm mount costs more and takes up more desk footprint, but it frees up the desk surface underneath and lets you push the laptop out of the way entirely when you want the desk clear.

Practical tips

Pair any stand with an external keyboard and mouse — without them, you are just looking at a laptop, not typing on one, which defeats the ergonomic point. Also check the maximum laptop size and weight a stand supports before buying if you use a larger 16-inch or 17-inch machine.

Bottom line

For most home offices, an adjustable aluminum riser with a wide, stable base is the sweet spot between price, sturdiness, and ergonomic benefit. Add a separate keyboard and mouse and you get the full neck-and-shoulder payoff a laptop stand is meant to provide.
