Why a vertical mouse

A standard mouse makes you rotate your forearm so your palm faces flat down on the desk. Hold that position for eight hours and the twist adds up. A vertical mouse tilts the grip upright so your hand rests in a "handshake" position, with the thumb on top and the forearm in a more neutral, less rotated posture. Many people who switch report less wrist and forearm discomfort, though it usually takes a few days to a week to get used to the different grip and pointer control.

Vertical angle: how upright is right?

Vertical mice are not all fully vertical. The tilt typically ranges from around 50 to 60 degrees, and some models let you adjust it. The Logitech MX Vertical, for example, uses a 57-degree angle. A steeper angle is closer to a true handshake but can feel less precise at first; a shallower angle is an easier transition from a regular mouse. If you are unsure, a model in the middle of that range is a sensible starting point.

What to check before buying

  • Hand size — this matters more for vertical mice than any other type. Some models are designed specifically for small to medium hands (Logitech's Lift is one) while others suit larger hands. A mouse that is too large or too small forces your fingers into an awkward position and defeats the purpose.
  • Left- or right-handed — vertical mice are shaped for one hand. Check that a left-handed version exists if you need one.
  • Connection — Bluetooth, a USB receiver, or both. Multi-device models that switch between a laptop and desktop with a button are handy if you use more than one computer at the same desk.
  • Rechargeable vs. replaceable battery — rechargeable is convenient but check whether the mouse can be used while charging.
  • Quiet clicks — useful if you take calls or share a room.

Practical setup tips

Keep the mouse close to the keyboard so you are not reaching sideways, and let your elbow rest at roughly 90 degrees with your shoulder relaxed. Move from the elbow and shoulder rather than flicking your wrist. If the pointer feels jumpy at first, lower the sensitivity (DPI) for the first week while your hand adapts.

Bottom line

For most people, a mid-range vertical mouse in the 50 to 60 degree range, sized for your hand and with the connection type you actually use, is the best value. Give it a full week before judging it, and see a clinician if wrist pain persists, since a mouse alone is not a treatment.
